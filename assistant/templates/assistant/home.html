Key Concepts

Essential Election Knowledge

Core concepts every voter should understand.

🗳️

Types of Elections

The US holds three main types: Primary (party candidate selection), General (final election between parties), and Special elections (fill vacant seats mid-term).

🗺️

Electoral College Basics

Each state has electors equal to its total Congressional seats. A candidate needs 270 of 538 electoral votes to win the presidency. Most states use winner-take-all rules.

📋

Voter Registration

Registration is the first step to participating. Requirements vary by state, but you generally must be a US citizen, 18+, and a state resident. Register at vote.gov.

⚖️

Your Voting Rights

The Constitution and federal law protect your right to vote free from discrimination. You have the right to accessible polling places, assistance if needed, and a provisional ballot if eligibility is questioned.

📬

Absentee & Mail-In Voting

Many states offer no-excuse absentee voting — you don't need a reason to vote by mail. Request your ballot early, complete it carefully, and return it before the deadline.

📊

How Ballots Are Counted

Election officials use a combination of hand-counting, optical scanners, and ballot tabulation machines. Multiple layers of audits and bipartisan oversight ensure accuracy and integrity.
